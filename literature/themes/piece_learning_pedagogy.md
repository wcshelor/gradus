# Theme brief: learning and teaching a piano piece

## Core question

What makes a particular passage difficult for a particular learner, how can a
teacher or system diagnose the cause, and which intervention best supports
durable, musical learning?

## Scope

Include research on new-piece learning, difficult-passage work, fingering,
reading, coordination, timing, memory, interpretation, feedback, error
management, practice organization, self-regulation, repertoire fit, and
teacher–student interaction. Instrument-general research is included when the
mechanism transfers plausibly to piano.

Exclude generic “practise slowly” advice without provenance from the empirical
catalog. Preserve practitioner literature in a separately identified expert
evidence layer. Treat score-level difficulty prediction as an input to this
theme, not as the complete learning problem.

## Search vocabulary

- piano practice; piano pedagogy; new piece; repertoire learning;
- difficult passage; practice strategy; error correction; feedback;
- fingering; bimanual coordination; timing; motor planning; biomechanics;
- sight-reading; eye–hand span; pattern recognition; score reading;
- memorization; performance cues; mental practice; retrieval; spacing;
- self-regulated learning; metacognition; teacher directives; studio teaching;
- expressive performance; modelling; demonstration; interpretation.

## Evidence hierarchy for this theme

1. Piano-specific intervention and observational studies.
2. Instrumental-learning systematic reviews and strong empirical studies.
3. General learning-science or motor-learning research with a stated transfer
   argument.
4. Expert pedagogical traditions, explicitly labelled.
5. Neuroscience and general music-benefit research as background only.

## Synthesis rule

Every proposed assistance mechanism should document:

- the diagnosed difficulty and alternative explanations;
- the learner evidence required;
- the intervention and its intended mechanism;
- the level and population of supporting evidence;
- how the learner or teacher can evaluate the result;
- boundaries, uncertainty, and any safety concern.

## Current gap

The literature is much stronger on describing expert practice and general
self-regulation than on experimentally matching a specific piano passage and
learner profile to a specific practice transformation. That gap is a promising
thesis contribution, but it requires cautious claims and teacher validation.
