# Idea bank

## Understanding a piece

- Point out passages that may deserve extra attention.
- Describe several possible reasons a passage might be demanding.
- Reveal recurring shapes, rhythms, harmonies, voices, or structural patterns.
- Compare similar passages and show what changes between them.
- Help a learner form a mental map of the piece before detailed practice.

## Supporting practice

- Suggest ways to approach a difficult passage from different angles.
- Create related warm-ups, reductions, variations, or stepping stones.
- Help divide a piece or passage into workable units.
- Offer alternative explanations for learners who respond to different kinds
  of language or representation.
- Help reconnect isolated work with the musical context of the whole piece.
- Support reflection on what improved, what remained unstable, and what to try
  next.

## Planning and continuity

- Help turn a longer-term goal into manageable sessions.
- Revisit material over time rather than treating practice as a single session.
- Adapt suggestions to available time, goals, confidence, or prior work.
- Preserve the learner's autonomy instead of presenting one supposedly correct
  routine.

## Interaction and input

- Let a learner describe what feels difficult in their own words.
- Compare score-derived expectations with learner or teacher observations.
- Explore whether performance recordings, MIDI, annotations, or practice logs
  would add useful evidence.
- Let a teacher review, adjust, or author guidance that the system can reuse.

## Pedagogical grounding

- Connect suggestions to documented teaching strategies or expert input.
- Show why a suggestion was made and what it is intended to develop.
- State uncertainty when the available evidence does not justify a diagnosis.
- Identify situations in which automated guidance should defer to a teacher.
- Examine musical understanding, expression, motivation, and healthy practice
  alongside technical execution.

## Evaluation possibilities

- Ask teachers to review relevance, playability, clarity, and appropriateness.
- Compare generic AI output with context-rich or pedagogically grounded output.
- Study whether learners can act on the guidance without extra explanation.
- Measure changes in confidence, practice efficiency, consistency, or
  performance where a suitable study design permits it.
- Record failure cases and misleading suggestions, not only successful demos.

## Open tensions

- General guidance versus personalization.
- Helpful structure versus learner autonomy.
- Score-based evidence versus embodied experience.
- Explainable rules versus flexible generation.
- Product usefulness versus research measurability.
- Breadth of possible assistance versus a thesis-sized evaluation.
